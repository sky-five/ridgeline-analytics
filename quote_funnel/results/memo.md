# Decision memo: the quote-to-bind drop and the candidate carrier

Generated by `python -m quote_funnel` from simulated data (2026-01-05 to the week of 2026-06-29); do not edit by hand.

## 1. Quote-to-bind fell in April. Do we act on it?

**No. The drop is a counting change, not a change in shoppers.** The dashboard's rate (binds per quoted request) went from 12.0% to 8.2% from the week of 2026-04-06, a 32% relative fall. That same week the request log went from 1.00 to 1.46 requests per session: edits and resubmits started being logged as new requests. Sessions (1,501 vs 1,493 a week) and binds (178 vs 175 a week) did not move. Counted per session, quote-to-bind is 12.0% before and 11.9% after: -0.10 points (95% CI -0.75 to +0.55, p = 0.77). The request count explains 98% of the drop.

Action: move the dashboard to the session-level definitions, keep requests per session as a logging health check with an alert on a step change, and note the break on any chart that spans it.

## 2. Should we add the candidate carrier to the panel?

**Yes, on these numbers.** The candidate priced 3,000 past auto sessions and quoted 2,302 of them. Scored with models fitted on the half's clicks and binds, adding it gives about 561 more binds a half-year (95% CI 515 to 621) and $58,988 more commission ($54,404 to $64,866). It would write about 967 policies, so roughly 406 of those come from carriers already on the panel. At an assumed $25,000 one-off onboarding cost that pays back in about 2.5 months.

Risks: the estimate gives the newcomer the panel's average brand pull, and its prices come from a rating sample, not live traffic. Launch on a share of auto traffic first and compare bound sessions per quoted session with the holdout.

Check against the simulator: replaying the same simulated shoppers with the candidate on the panel gives 534 more binds, $56,060 more commission and 866 policies for the candidate. A real team would not have this; it is here to show the method lands near the truth.
