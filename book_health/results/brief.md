# Provincial book health: the brief

Simulated home and auto book, 2022-01 to 2026-06. Last 12 months unless said.

## What the numbers say

- **The book:** 22,110 policies in force, $48.4M written, loss ratio 64.2%, 87.5% of renewals kept.
- **Growth:** British Columbia has 5.3% more policies in force than a year ago; Ontario -2.5%.
- **Ontario auto paid for its rate increase in customers.** Renewals from 2025-04 took 14.0% on average (was 4.0%); retention fell from 87.2% to 76.7% in the year after.
- **The 2024-07-22 hail in Alberta** cost $1.4M across 61 home claims: Alberta home's loss ratio was 550.8% that month and 107.5% for the year (year before: 51.2%). The 10.0% increase that followed moved retention from 90.9% to 85.1%.
- **Loss ratio by province** runs from 56.2% (Ontario) to 87.0% (Nova Scotia). The small provinces swing year to year on a few large claims; read them over two or three years, not one.
- **Digital customers leave more:** 78.3% renew, against 89.3% through brokers.

## Two cautions

- **The last three months look better than they are.** 165 claims ($2.3M) from those months were not reported by 2026-06-30. With them the loss ratio is 63.1%, not 43.8%.
- **A 12-month average is a good retention forecast until prices move.** Back-tested over every month and province, the trailing 12 rule misses by 1.80 points on average (last month: 2.25, trailing 3: 1.82). In Ontario's first six months after the increase the 12-month average was 4.1 points too hopeful, the 3-month average 2.4.

## So what

- Plan on 4,794 of the 5,475 renewals due in the next three months staying, and switch to the 3-month average in any province or product that takes a rate change above the usual.
- Price each rate action against the customers it will cost: in Ontario auto each extra point of increase cost 1.1 points of retention.
- Report the latest quarter's loss ratio with the late-claims allowance next to it, or not at all.
